Dear GitLab team,

A knowledge graph is only as valuable as it is legible. The hard part was never storing the relationships between code, dependencies, issues, and the people who touch them - it's turning that dense web into something a person can actually read, navigate, and trust at a glance. That's a design problem before it's a data one, and making complexity feel calm and obvious is the exact work I've spent my career on.

I'm a product designer and design engineer with 13 years of craft - visual and brand design first, then UX, and now AI-native product work. I design in Figma and ship in code (React, Tailwind, Astro), so I test ideas against real constraints instead of static mockups. My clearest recent proof is public and shipped: the Article 50 UI Pattern Kit, an open-source (MIT) React component library I designed and built for EU AI Act transparency - four WCAG 2.1 AA components with a passing axe audit and a live demo (github.com/sotosili/article50-kit). GitLab's all-remote, build-in-the-open culture is how I already work: in code, in public, with the reasoning written down.

My read is that a knowledge graph is where GitLab's product gets genuinely intelligent - it's the substrate both a person and an AI feature reason over. If it feeds AI, then legibility and trust become the whole game: showing not just what's connected, but why, and how sure the system is. Keeping a human legibly in control as the platform does more of the thinking is the interaction I care about most, and it's exactly the seat this role sits on. I would love to talk.
