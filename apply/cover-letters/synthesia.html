Dear Synthesia team,

The hardest design problem in AI video isn't the interface around it - it's making something generative and non-deterministic feel controllable, trustworthy, and safe to the person creating it. Synthetic media that people can trust is exactly the problem this role sits on, and it's the one I've built my work around.

I'm a product designer and design engineer with 13 years of craft, working AI-native - I design in Figma and build in code (React, Tailwind, Astro), with Claude and Cursor as native parts of my process, on a spine of accessibility and responsible AI. I don't stop at static screens. My clearest recent proof is public and shipped: the Article 50 UI Pattern Kit, an open-source (MIT) React library I designed and built for EU AI Act transparency - AI disclosure, content labelling, an emotion-recognition notice, and a human-in-the-loop review gate, every component WCAG 2.1 AA - plus a live demo where an AI discloses itself, streams its reasoning, shows a real confidence meter, and hands low-confidence calls to a person (article50-kit.pages.dev). It's exactly the "make AI feel trustworthy" problem, solved in real, accessible code.

My read is that Synthesia's edge won't come from model quality alone - it will come from whether creators feel in control and audiences feel they can trust what they are watching. Article 50 makes that a legal duty from August; I think the winning products will make it a feature instead of a disclaimer. That is the seat I want: designing AI-native products where clarity, control, and trust are the interface. I would love to talk.
