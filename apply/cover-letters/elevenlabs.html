Dear ElevenLabs team,

Voice is the most intimate interface there is - which makes it the hardest to get right. The moment audio becomes generative, the design question stops being "does it sound good" and becomes "does the person trust it, control it, and understand what's synthetic". That's the problem this role sits on, and it's the one I've oriented my work around.

I'm a product designer and design engineer with 13 years of craft, working AI-native - I design in Figma and build in code (React, Tailwind, Astro), with Claude and Cursor as native parts of my process, on a spine of accessibility and responsible AI. Voice and audio are where accessibility stops being a checkbox and becomes the product itself - and where EU AI Act transparency, what is AI-generated and with whose consent, becomes real design work. My clearest proof is public and shipped: the Article 50 UI Pattern Kit, an open-source (MIT) React library I built for exactly that - AI disclosure, content labelling, consent and human-in-the-loop review, every component WCAG 2.1 AA - with a live demo of the accountable-AI loop at article50-kit.pages.dev.

My read is that ElevenLabs' edge won't come from voice quality alone - it will come from whether creators and listeners feel in control of something this powerful, and clear about what's real. Content labelling and consent are exactly what Article 50 makes a duty this August; the products that win will make that a feature, not a disclaimer. That is the seat I want: designing AI-native products where clarity and trust are the interface. I would love to talk.
